# English: patterns and reading load

These checks adapt the English Humanizer's scope. They identify possible editing problems,
not authorship. Change a passage for a concrete benefit, not because a word appears on a list.

## Locate the problem

| Family | Look for | Preserve |
|---|---|---|
| Staging | Empty contrasts, run-up openers, pseudo-profound sayings, unraised objections, closers that only repeat | Useful distinctions, real objections, orientation, emphasis and the writer's position |
| Mechanical rhythm | Forced triads, repeated openings, dashes joining everything, redundant hedges, awkward compound labels | Distinct items, intentional repetition, established terminology, qualifiers with different meanings |
| Inflation | Generic praise, inflated significance, vague connections, unsupported -ing riders, sales language, borrowed authority, roundabout copulas | Attributed claims, actual evaluations, sourced relationships, comparison and capability versus completion |
| Formatting | Unhelpful bold labels, decorative headings, inconsistent quotes or hyphens | Target style, definitions, meaningful lists, headings/anchors and protected technical content |
| Residue | Chat greetings/offers in standalone text, repeated disclaimers, heading echoes, editing-process narration | Letters' courtesy, information limits, methods, source notes, change logs and reviewer replies |
| Reader/context | Rebuilding background the addressee visibly already knows | Background needed by a standalone reader; abstracts and sections may serve different reading tasks |

Do not ban em/en dashes, title case, curly quotes, formal words or passive voice. Follow the
target's style and ordinary lexical usage; preserve range dashes and established hyphenation.
Do not remove an attributed opinion or unnamed-source claim merely to make it sound firmer.
When its support is material but missing, flag that limitation outside the edited prose.

## Trace difficult relationships

- Find the subject and finite verb through nested relative clauses and interruptions. Can
  the reader tell which noun each clause modifies without rereading?
- Unpack noun strings, prepositional chains and nominalizations only as far as the source
  establishes their relations. Sometimes an explicit clause is clearer than a compressed noun.
- Check the attachment of participial phrases and the referents of this/which/it. A trailing
  -ing phrase may add a result, purpose or evaluation; do not silently turn one into another.
- If splitting a sentence, carry over restrictions, exceptions, temporal order, negation,
  modal force and attribution. Useful long sentences can stay long.
- Use an active construction when its actor is known and this improves the passage. Unknown
  or unimportant actors and topic continuity can justify a passive. Do not invent “the system”
  or “the researchers” to fill an empty subject slot.

## Examples and boundaries

**Nested clauses.** “The samples that the technicians who were unaware of group assignments
had labeled were excluded only if their storage records were incomplete.” One option:
“Technicians who were unaware of group assignments had labeled the samples. Samples were
excluded only if their storage records were incomplete.” Preserve the blinded technicians,
prior labeling and necessary condition. Do not claim every incomplete record led to exclusion.

**Layered limits.** “Under low-light conditions, the method may reduce error; this has not been
validated outdoors.” The condition, possible effect and validation boundary all matter.
“The method reduces error” is not an equivalent simplification.

**Copulas and quantities.** Replacing “serves as” with “is” can help, but “can provide” does
not mean “provides”, and “over 3,000” does not mean “3,000”. Keep numbers and their comparisons.

**Purposeful structure.** “We checked the logs, the receipt and the signed copy” contains three
different items. “It failed during receipt, not transmission” corrects a specific distinction.
Neither needs to be shortened merely because it matches a pattern.

Do not add a writer's reaction, personal experience, named source or missing technical detail
to make prose feel specific. Keep stable terms rather than rotating synonyms for variety.
